Step 1 — choose a seasonal model

Each model is shown from its own most recent init: NOAA CFSv2 from December 2026; C3S Multi-Model Mean, CMCC SPS4, DWD GCFS2.2, ECCC canSIPS, ECMWF SEAS5, METFR System 9, NOAA SFSv1 and UKMO GloSeas6-GC5.1 from November 2026; NOAA GEFSv12, BOM ACCESS-S2 and JMA CPS4 are on the November 2026 init with the ENSO plume, and publish no snowfall field. Pick a model and you will be taken to its list of variables — accumulated snowfall over the continental United States, ONI and RONI sea surface temperature plumes for the four ENSO regions, and the 10 hPa zonal-mean zonal wind (the stratospheric polar vortex proxy) against the ERA5 1991–2020 climatology. The polar cap section is the same stratosphere from the other side: the 60–90°N geopotential height anomaly as a time-height picture, in front of NOAA’s CORe reanalysis, for the ECMWF SEAS5 and for NOAA’s GEFSv12.

NOAA CFSv2

NOAA CFSv2 · 40-member ensemble · init 1 Dec 2026 · read directly from NCEP CPC. The ENSO plume is built from CPC's own published Nino indices, so it carries the member spread. NOAA publishes no 10 hPa winds, so this model has snowfall panels only — no zonal-wind panel.

NOAA GEFSv12

NOAA GEFS v12 sub-seasonal ensemble · 35 days, from the 00Z cycle, which is the one that carries the extended range · the ensemble mean of all 31 members (the control and the thirty perturbations), which the archive serves at every one of those 35 days, so the mean does not change membership half way through the chart · the polar cap's geopotential height anomaly on the same scale as the reanalysis

Snowfall is accumulated snow in inches. Anomalies are computed against each model's own hindcast climate. The 10 hPa charts show every ensemble member, the ensemble mean, the ERA5 daily climatology with 10–90th and 30–70th percentile bands, the 1959–2020 record bounds and the model's own climate mean where one could be built.
