The ECMWF is carried over to the map instead of queued for it

The owner asked why the map needs more models when the static pages
already have them.  The answer was two bugs and one piece of data that
was already in the repository.

THE INDEX DROPPED MODELS.  write_index() wrote the picker from THIS
run's summary alone, so every rebuild dropped every model whose GRIB
was not on disk - even though its rasters and manifest were committed
and sitting in docs/data/.  That is how the ECMWF's export was lost
(commit c2751cf deleted it) and why the map kept needing the queue.
The index now keeps every model whose export is on disk with its
rasters beside it, and the "is it there?" check is separate from the
list - the same rule the queue learned today.

THE ECMWF'S FIELD WAS ALREADY IN THE REPOSITORY.  The SEAS5 fetch
wrote data/seas5_<init>_snow_em.npz (the ensemble mean, six lead
months, the model's own 1 degree grid) and _snow_hc.npz (the model's
own hindcast climate), and BOTH ARE COMMITTED TO GIT.  tools/
carry_over_seas5.py writes them into data/models/ecmwf-seas5/ in the
convention the exporter and the panel renderer already read, so both
products are built from them with no queue, no request and no waiting.
The field is bit-for-bit the same: compared cell by cell against the
committed source the largest difference anywhere is 0.000e+00 in, and
the static panel re-rendered from it differs from the published one in
187 of 2,167,620 pixels - isolated single pixels on a contour edge.

THE EXTENSION WAS DECODED ON A CONUS CROP.  The extension past the
sixth lead month is FETCHED globally but was decoded on the 20-56N /
134-56W box the panels use, so the map got a 36x78 fragment it could
not interpolate onto its own grid.  read_snow() takes crop=False for
the global case, and interp_to() now accepts a pair of target AXES as
well as a pair of points.

The ECMWF is now on the map with seven lead months - the seventh is the
sub-daily extension out to May 2027 - and with its own hindcast
climate, so its anomaly is drawn.
